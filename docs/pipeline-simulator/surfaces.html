The frontend has no chart library. Its charts are inline SVG (quota-curve, workspace-token-timeline, operations-trend). These seven follow that pattern. "First pass" and "rework" follow TokenUsageKindPolicy: coding is first pass, and a fix round (every coding execution after the first) is rework. Reviews are split by round: the first review of a card is first pass, later ones are rework. The token analysis keeps every review under one kind, so SIM-1 adds the round number to review rows. Totals still reconcile with the token analysis.

03 Where it lives

The simulator is a project hub section next to "Throughput & cycle time" (cycle-time in PROJECT_RAIL_ITEMS, project-shell.config.ts). Its key is simulation and its label is "Pipeline world", at #/projects/<slug>/simulation. It opens in live mode. The view is full-bleed (rule R2). The world takes the upper part, and the charts and the card ribbon scroll below it. Saved what-if runs and comparisons belong to the project and are listed in the section's side panel.

It is project-scoped because the gate, the window, the strategy and the conflict record are per project. Coding and review slots are shared between projects on one host. A run therefore takes the other projects' recorded load on those slots as a given, and the section says so. A workspace view with all projects in one world is a later step, after the project view has passed its replay check.

05 Judging personas

Dispatcher. The dispatcher persona Dossier (docs/dispatcher-personas/) plans a replay harness on recorded waves (its slice DS-4) to judge strategies in shadow. That harness and this engine would do the same thing twice. The recommendation is one engine. The dispatcher's strategies plug into its "which cards may run together" choice through DispatchPlanPolicy. A strategy is judged by replaying a recorded wave with it: cards needing a sync, cards per hour, lead time and rework USD against the strategy that was live (SIM-7). The wave model already shows why the owner needs this. A perfect forecast at the level of a series cuts the cards needing a sync from 16 to about 1. But it lets only one card of each overlapping series run at a time, so the wave drains in 26.9 hours instead of 8.6. The useful strategies lie between these two extremes, and finding them is what the replay is for.

Line guardian. The guardian of docs/red-test-isolation/ decides quarantine, revert or fix forward and which area waits. The engine calls RedTestTriagePolicy for the red-after-integration loop, so a replay of 22 and 23 November with the guardian's policy shows how many cards would have kept flowing. The guardian's six criteria can then be scored on simulated incidents before it goes live.

Neither replay calls a model. A persona step that used a model in the recording is replayed from its recorded output.
