Dispatcher personas

A new role in the persona team decides which Ready card starts when, what runs in parallel and what is chained, and for packaging personas how findings are cut into cards. Several dispatcher personas with their own rules compete on a replay of real waves, in shadow and alternately live. The owner ranks them by one weighted value.

01 Scope

An analysis of 17 and 18 November found that about 40 % of Claude usage was rework. 75 of 95 repeat coding runs since 15 November followed a review that had passed. In each case the delivery conflicted with develop at integration, the agent got a round to include develop, the new delivery went through the full review again, and by then develop had moved again. For Agent Studio the median batch gate latency is 133 minutes (p95 598), 48 % of batches are green, and every delivered card costs 1.9 full suite runs.

The owner decided the following between 20 and 22 November 2026. This Dossier takes those decisions as fixed and does not reopen them:

  • A merge conflict is an orchestration error, and specifically an error of the dispatcher. Selecting and ordering cards must prevent conflicts first. Cheaper conflict handling comes second.
  • Integrate first, then test, is the default. Only the automated tests move behind integration, and the model review aspects stay in front of it. This is configurable, and the fast mode is the default. For Agent Studio, develop and Stage get a light base suite and are then deployed. Only a Production release gets the full suite.
  • Efficiency comes before lead time.
  • The selection knows several strategies and applies them. Strategies belong to the persona and role model, and personas compete with each other.
  • The owner reviews personas by criteria that combine into one weighted value. The weights start with optimistic defaults, and the owner adjusts them afterwards.
  • A persona whose strategy is packaging may recut cards, merging overlapping findings into one larger card or into a chain. Every persona has its own rules.

This is a concept and changes no product code. It defines the role, five starter personas, the competition, the criteria and their weights, the two pages, and how cards are cut. It then splits the work into eleven slices that can be promoted to cards. Its main evidence is a replay of the 57 playthrough cards of 17 November. The replay was rebuilt from git, and its data and script are in replay/.

02 In one paragraph

The dispatcher is a persona role whose decisions are pure rules, not model sessions. Each dispatcher persona names a versioned strategy, which is a rule set evaluated in the orchestration decision library. The strategy gets typed facts: the Ready queue with owner pins and dependencies, the free slots, a touch forecast per card, and the conflict ledger. It returns a dispatch plan: an order, holds, chains, and admit or serialize for every free slot. Exactly one persona per project is live and its plan feeds the pick-gate. All other personas run in shadow. They compute their plan on the same facts at the same moment, and the plan is recorded but never applied. Models are used only for recut texts and the optional weekly narrative, so a dispatcher's own token cost stays small. After every wave the real changed files are known, so every persona's plan can be scored on the same cards: conflicts per 100 integrated cards, tokens per accepted card, share of rework, forecast accuracy, parallelism used, lead time, the persona's own cost, and the quality of its decision log. The weighted sum of these is multiplied by a parallelism gate, so that no persona can win by leaving slots empty. Replayed on the 17 November wave, today's order would have run 31 overlapping pairs in parallel and sent 20 cards into a develop sync. In the real history 21 cards needed one. An Area warden that uses forecast modules instead of today's keyword areas would have sent 7. Even a planner that knows the real files used only 62 % of four slots on this wave without a conflict.

04 Role

Charter

Dispatcher. Decide which Ready card starts when, what runs alongside what, and what is chained, so that cards integrate without conflicts at the lowest token cost per accepted card while using the parallel slots the work allows. Explain every decision. Never change what a card asks for. Never move a card into Progress, Review, Human review or Done. Never lower a priority or move an owner pin. Never remove a dependency set by the owner, a Dossier or another persona. Never raise the slot count.

How a dispatcher runs

Dispatch decisions are frequent and cheap, so they must not be model sessions. A slot frees several times an hour, and a persona occurrence is a five-minute session that runs at most seven to ten times a week. A dispatcher persona is therefore a descriptor that names a strategy: a versioned rule set with parameters, evaluated by a pure policy in orchestration-decisions/, as the repository requires for orchestration rules. The host collects the facts, calls the policy, and applies the returned plan. The persona's sessions do the rest: the weekly report and, for packaging personas, the text of recut proposals.

Rights

Rights are capability-catalogue operations granted per persona (PT-D15, PT-D16). The tier follows the persona team rule. T1 is a bounded, reversible action under a standing grant. T2 is anything that bypasses a check, and it needs an explicit, time-boxed owner grant with a use cap (PT-D17). Shadow personas hold only observe and dispatch.shadow.record.

Reporting duty

  • Every decision writes one log entry (PersonaLogEntryV1 once PT-18 is delivered): decision id, strategy and revision, the rule that fired, the digest of the facts, the candidates it passed over and why, the expected overlap, and a templated reason of at most 280 characters. Shadow decisions write the same entry, marked shadow.
  • Every wave gets a model-free summary item in the persona feed once its last card integrates: cards, conflicts and their attribution, holds over 4 hours, chains, recuts, forecast accuracy, parallelism used.
  • Every week a report from the template dispatcher-v1, composed deterministically. One optional model call within the allowance writes the narrative.
  • Immediately: every conflict attributed to the live persona becomes a feed item with importance from a fact floor of 60, so it reaches the magazine.

05 Starter personas

Five personas, each with one strategy and its own rules. All of them respect owner pins, urgent, priority classes, dependsOn, exclusive runs and the slot count. Unless noted, all of them are work-conserving: when the head card must wait, they look further down the queue for an admissible card rather than leaving a slot empty.

Queue keeper · baseline

Strategy lane-entry-v1. Today's behaviour, kept as the control group: owner pins first, then newest arrival, and any card is admitted when a slot is free (unknown scope is optimistic).

Inputs Ready lane, pins, dependsOn, free slots. Model none. Rights none beyond observe. When it is live, its plan equals today's pick order.

Rules No holds, no chains, no recut. It never retires: it is the reference every other persona is measured against, and the fallback when a live persona is paused or exceeds the guard.

Area warden

Strategy one-per-area-v1. Never two cards of one area at a time.

Inputs Touch forecast, reduced to modules: frontend/src/app/features/<x>, frontend/src/app/<x>, frontend/e2e/<x>, backend/Features/<X>, orchestration-decisions, runner. Not detectedAreas.

Rules Hub files (Program.cs, RoutePermissionCatalog.cs, task.model.ts, extendable) do not define an area. A card without a forecast has the area unknown, and only one such card runs at a time. A card forecast over more than 3 modules runs exclusive. Model none.

Surveyor

Strategy file-overlap-v1. Estimates the touched files per card and separates only on real overlap.

Inputs Forecast files with confidence. Optionally, a model-assisted forecast at Ready entry (rule parameter forecast: model-assisted, about 3,000 tokens per card, charged to the persona).

Rules Serialize when two forecasts share at least one non-hub file. A low-confidence forecast (no cited path) falls back to module level. Skip ahead to fill slots with disjoint cards. Sets PredictedScope so the existing pick-gate rule finally fires.

Historian

Strategy ledger-learned-v1. Learns from the conflict ledger.

Inputs The ledger (card pairs, partner card, files, base SHA), the real changed files of delivered cards, the forecast.

Rules Surveyor rules plus: a module that took part in a conflict within 14 days is hot, and only one card at a time may touch it. Two cards that collided never run together again in their recovery rounds. A source whose earlier wave had a mean partner count above 5 is chained by default. With an empty ledger it behaves exactly like the Surveyor. Model none.

Packer

Strategy package-then-chain-v1. Packages overlapping findings before the start.

Inputs Forecast, the source wave (findings and their source card), a size prior per source kind (median real code files of earlier delivered cards from the same kind of source), the ledger.

Rules Merge findings from one source into one card when they share at least 2 non-hub forecast files, at most 3 findings and a size prior of at most 25 code files. Chain the rest of an overlapping cluster in source order. Split proposals for collection cards that touch more than 3 modules. Never package across projects or Dossiers. Then dispatch like the Surveyor. Model recut title and prompt, within its allowance. Rights dispatch.recut.*.

Area precision

enrichmentReport.detectedAreas is not precise enough to separate cards. DetectTaskAreas looks for substrings such as card, ui, state, service or api in the whole prompt. On the source text of the 55 wave cards that have one, it tagged 53 cards frontend and 48 backend, with 3.6 areas per card on average. As an overlap test it flags 1,482 card pairs, while 342 pairs really share a code file. That is a precision of 0.23. A warden that uses these areas runs no conflicts, but it falls back to almost sequential work: on the replay it uses 27 % of four slots and needs 53 rounds instead of 15. The same matcher tagged this concept card, which changes only documents, as frontend and git and attached the design-token and git rules to its prompt. Detected areas remain useful for what they were built for, which is choosing enrichment blocks. Separating cards needs file paths.

Four findings follow from the replay.

  1. Separating cards on a forecast cuts the cards needing a develop sync by 45 to 65 %, at a lead time close to the conflict-free bound. The bound is 23 rounds against 26 to 27 for the practical personas.
  2. The forecast-module Area warden is the most robust. It is first or tied first in all four runs, needs no model, and needs no recut rights.

Weighted value

Each criterion becomes a score from 0 to 100 within one comparison set: all personas on the same cards in the same window, with the baseline always included. The best value in the set scores 100 and the worst scores 0. Forecast accuracy and parallelism are already ratios and score as 100 × the ratio. The weighted value is the weighted mean of the scores, multiplied by the parallelism gate, min(1, parallelism ÷ 0.8).

value = Σ (weight_c × score_c) ÷ Σ weight_c × min(1, parallelism ÷ gate)      gate = 0.8

The gate is necessary, not cosmetic. With a plain weighted sum, the keyword Area warden wins the replay with 81 points, by running almost sequentially. Conflicts, tokens and rework all reward an empty slot. With the gate it drops to 45, and the warden that uses forecast modules (65) and the Packer (67) lead. A value is shown only with at least 40 integrated cards per persona in the window. Below that the page says "too few cards", as the persona team does for rates.

Optimistic defaults

The defaults are optimistic in two ways. They trust every measure from the first wave on, without a discount for thin data or for estimated shadow tokens. They also put 65 of 100 points on efficiency (conflicts, tokens, rework) and only 5 on lead time, as if the forecasts were already good. The first review is expected to correct them. Shadow token estimates, for example, may deserve less weight than real tokens.

Changing the weights

The review page has a weights panel. The owner enters numbers that must add up to 100, and the panel shows the remainder as they type. The gate threshold sits next to them (default 0.8, range 0.5 to 1). Saving creates a new weight revision with the owner as author, the time, and an optional reason. Raw criterion values are stored per persona and window, so every ranking is recomputed under the new revision, and the history can show the ranking under any earlier revision. Personas can read the weights but cannot change them. Managers cannot change them either. The setting lives in the workspace settings as dispatch.review.weights and is written only by the owner.

10 Cut of cards

A dispatcher can only order what it is given. Three cuts cause most of the overlap.

Waves from one source

Playthroughs, review-finding sweeps and the scout create many cards for one area at once. On 17 November three playthroughs created 57 cards in one minute with no dependency, and 33 of them formed one overlap cluster. Rules:

  • A source that emits more than 5 cards writes a wave id and, per finding, the cited paths it already knows. The playthrough cards cite files with line numbers in their cause field. The cards land in Backlog with creationSource and wave id.
  • The live dispatcher plans the wave as a whole before any card enters Ready: packages (Packer only), chains for dense clusters, and interleaving so that sparse cards fill parallel slots while chains run.
  • Source prompts gain one instruction: group findings that share a cause file into one card, and say which findings must land before which.

Cards that are too large

IN-13 (AGT-3921) changed 66 files, of which 29 are code files, and merged develop six times. The finding text gave no warning. Over the wave, real code files correlate at 0.08 with the number of acceptance criteria, at 0.12 with the word count, and at 0.10 with the forecast size. Size must therefore come from a size prior per source kind, the median real code files of earlier delivered cards from that kind of source. On this wave that is 17 for playthrough product gaps and 5 for review findings. Rules:

  • A card whose forecast spans more than 3 modules, or whose source prior exceeds 25 code files, runs exclusive or gets a split proposal before the start.
  • When a delivery's real change exceeds 40 code files, the ledger records it and the size prior is updated. The dispatcher never recuts a card that has started.
  • Packages respect the prior. In the first replay draft the Packer bundled IN-13 with two other incident cards because their forecasts looked small, which would have produced a card of about 90 code files. The size prior rule prevents this.

Collection cards of small fixes

DP-18 (AGT-3908) collected three small deployment fixes and touched 26 code files with 23 partner cards. RV-20 to RV-22 (AGT-3943 to AGT-3945) collected 6 to 7 review findings each, touching 5 to 15 code files with 2 to 3 partners. Spread is harmless when the modules are cold and harmful when one of them is hot. Rule: a collection card that touches a hot module is split by module, and the hot part is chained behind the cluster. A collection card in cold modules stays whole, because one review is cheaper than seven.

12 Alternatives

DS-D1 · Form of a dispatcher

A. Model session that orders the lane

Like the chief of staff, a periodic session reads Ready and writes an order. It is far too slow and too costly for a decision that is due every time a slot frees, and its decisions cannot be replayed deterministically.

B. Persona names a pure strategy (recommended)

A versioned rule set in orchestration-decisions/, selected and parameterised by the persona descriptor. It is replayable, explainable, and costs no tokens in the hot path. Sessions only write recut texts and reports.

C. Project setting without personas

A scheduler mode per project. It is cheap, but it contradicts the owner's decision that strategies belong to personas that compete.

DS-D2 · Where the order lives

A. Write the lane order

Simple, but it collides with owner drags, the 999 pin sentinel and the five-move limit of managers.

B. Separate dispatch plan (recommended)

The live persona's plan is a record next to the lane. The pick-gate reads it. Pins and urgent stay hard constraints. The board shows plan position and hold reasons.

C. Advisory only

Plans are shown and never applied. That is the same as shadow forever, so conflicts never fall.

DS-D3 · Rights and tiers

A. Everything T1

Fast, but removing owner dependencies or admitting past a check would need no grant.

B. Bounded T1, T2 for every bypass (recommended)

Order, hold for at most 24 hours, own dependency edges, exclusive and bounded recut are T1. Removing others' edges, recutting owner cards and admitting past a check are T2.

C. Proposals only

Every hold or chain waits for the owner. The owner becomes the dispatcher.

DS-D4 · Touch forecast

A. Keyword areas

Exists, but has a pair precision of 0.23 and drives a warden to 27 % slot use.

B. Shared model-free forecast (recommended)

Cited paths, co-change expansion from a dated index, a module map, and confidence, with real files recorded at delivery. Pair F1 is 0.52 to 0.56 at zero tokens.

C. Model estimate for every card

Possibly more accurate, but costs about 3,000 tokens per card for everybody. It is better offered as a persona option whose cost the persona carries.

DS-D5 · Conflict record

A. Latest report per card

Today. No history, no partner, no attribution.

B. Append-only ledger with partner attribution (recommended)

One entry per conflict, semantic conflict or develop sync: card, partner cards that changed the conflicted files between base and tip, files, SHAs, the admitting decision ids, live or shadow. Backfilled for 30 days from git.

C. Derive from git on demand

No store, but slow, and the admitting decision is lost.

DS-D6 · Competition order

A. Alternating live from the start

Real numbers, but a weak persona costs real rework from the first day.

B. Replay, then shadow, then alternating live (recommended)

Replay is free and immediate. Shadow is near free and uses real decision-time facts. Only the top two go live, by wave window per project, behind a guard.

C. Replay and shadow only

Safe, but tokens and agent behaviour in chains are never measured for real.

DS-D7 · Criteria and value

A. Plain weighted sum

Simple, but the replay shows that it ranks a nearly sequential warden first (81).

B. Weighted sum with parallelism gate (recommended)

Min-max scores within one comparison set, default weights 30, 25, 10, 10, 10, 5, 5, 5, and a gate at 0.8 of the conflict-free bound.

C. Lexicographic

Conflicts first, then tokens. Transparent, but the owner cannot trade anything off, which contradicts weights the owner adjusts.

DS-D8 · Recut and source cut

A. Only the owner recuts

Safe and slow. Waves of 57 cards arrive in one minute.

B. Packer recuts automated waves, sources emit wave and paths (recommended)

Packages and chains for unstarted automated-source cards with a 24-hour undo, size from the source prior, and split proposals for oversized and hot collection cards.

C. Sources cut themselves

Needed in part (the prompt instruction), but a playthrough cannot see the rest of the board or the ledger.

DS-D9 · First live persona

A. Queue keeper stays live until shadow ends

No change in conflicts for several weeks.

B. Area warden on modules first, the rest in shadow (recommended)

Most robust in the replay, model-free, no recut rights needed. It goes live after its shadow gate. The Packer stays in shadow with proposals only until two waves prove its tokens.

C. Packer live directly

Top value on the unit replay, but its token gain is a proxy, and it is worse than the warden in one robustness run.

13 Recommendation

Recommended default

Build the dispatcher as a persona role whose personas name pure strategies (DS-D1 B), with a separate dispatch plan (DS-D2 B), bounded T1 rights and T2 for every bypass (DS-D3 B). Give every persona the same model-free touch forecast (DS-D4 B) and an append-only conflict ledger with partner attribution (DS-D5 B). Let personas compete by replay, then shadow, then alternating live (DS-D6 B), ranked by a weighted value with a parallelism gate (DS-D7 B). Let the Packer recut automated waves within bounds, while sources emit waves and paths (DS-D8 B). Put the forecast-module Area warden live first (DS-D9 B).

The most important reason: on the 17 November wave, separating on forecast modules would have cut the cards needing a develop sync from 20 to 7, at no token cost and with a lead time 4 rounds above what a conflict-free plan allows.

Expected effect on the scope numbers. Replayed, the practical personas cut rounds per card from 1.35 to 1.05 to 1.12, which is 17 to 22 % fewer rounds. Under integrate-first every avoided sync also avoids a re-review. This is the share of the 40 % rework that comes from integration. Rework from failed reviews is not touched by a dispatcher.

DS-D1 · Form of a dispatcher

Is a dispatcher a model session, a pure strategy chosen by a persona, or a project setting?

  • Model session that orders the lane
  • Persona names a pure strategy in the decision library (recommended)
  • Project setting without personas

Recommended: B. Decisions are frequent, must be replayable and explainable, and must cost no tokens. Sessions write only recut texts and reports.

  • Separate dispatch plan read by the pick-gate; pins and urgent stay hard (recommended)
  • Advisory only

Recommended: B. Owner drags keep their meaning, and plans can be compared between personas.

Which dispatcher actions are T1 and which need an explicit T2 grant?

  • Bounded T1 (order, hold of at most 24 hours, own edges, exclusive, bounded recut); T2 for every bypass (recommended)
  • Proposals only

Recommended: B, with the bounds in Rights.

Recommended: B. Without the partner and the decision, a conflict cannot be charged to a dispatcher.
