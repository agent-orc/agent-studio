Dispatcher personas

A new role in the persona team decides which Ready card starts when, what runs in parallel and what is chained, and for packaging personas how findings are cut into cards. Several dispatcher personas with their own rules compete on a replay of real waves, in shadow and alternately live. The owner ranks them by one weighted value.

01 In one paragraph

This page holds what the owner decides. The four chapters listed above carry the detail; README.md lists every file.

The dispatcher is a persona role whose decisions are pure rules, not model sessions. Each dispatcher persona names a versioned strategy, which is a rule set evaluated in the orchestration decision library. The strategy gets typed facts: the Ready queue with owner pins and dependencies, the free slots, a touch forecast per card, and the conflict ledger. It returns a dispatch plan: an order, holds, chains, and admit or serialize for every free slot. Exactly one persona per project is live and its plan feeds the pick-gate. All other personas run in shadow. They compute their plan on the same facts at the same moment, and the plan is recorded but never applied. Models are used only for recut texts and the optional weekly narrative, so a dispatcher's own token cost stays small. After every wave the real changed files are known, so every persona's plan can be scored on the same cards: conflicts per 100 integrated cards, tokens per accepted card, share of rework, forecast accuracy, parallelism used, lead time, the persona's own cost, and the quality of its decision log. The weighted sum of these is multiplied by a parallelism gate, so that no persona can win by leaving slots empty. Replayed on the 17 November wave with instant integration, today's order would have run 31 overlapping pairs in parallel and sent 20 cards into a develop sync. In the real history 21 cards needed one. An Area warden that uses forecast modules instead of today's keyword areas would have sent 7. Even a planner that knows the real files used only 62 % of four slots on this wave without a conflict. Separation helps only if it covers the whole exposure window. A card stays exposed until it integrates, but today's pick-gate compares a candidate only with cards that are still coding. With an integration lag of one card duration, a check against running cards leaves 29 to 35 of the 57 cards needing a sync under every strategy. A check against every started card that has not integrated yet brings the forecast personas down to 12 to 15.

02 Alternatives

DS-D1 · Form of a dispatcher

A. Model session that orders the lane

Like the chief of staff, a periodic session reads Ready and writes an order. It is far too slow and too costly for a decision that is due every time a slot frees, and its decisions cannot be replayed deterministically.

B. Persona names a pure strategy (recommended)

A versioned rule set in orchestration-decisions/, selected and parameterised by the persona descriptor. It is replayable, explainable, and costs no tokens in the hot path. Sessions only write recut texts and reports.

C. Project setting without personas

A scheduler mode per project. It is cheap, but it contradicts the owner's decision that strategies belong to personas that compete.

DS-D2 · Where the order lives

A. Write the lane order

Simple, but it collides with owner drags, the 999 pin sentinel and the five-move limit of managers.

B. Separate dispatch plan (recommended)

The live persona's plan is a record next to the lane. The pick-gate reads it. Pins and urgent stay hard constraints. The board shows plan position and hold reasons.

C. Advisory only

Plans are shown and never applied. That is the same as shadow forever, so conflicts never fall.

DS-D3 · Rights and tiers

A. Everything T1

Fast, but removing owner dependencies or admitting past a check would need no grant.

B. Bounded T1, T2 for every bypass (recommended)

Order, hold for at most 24 hours, own dependency edges, exclusive and bounded recut are T1. Removing others' edges, recutting owner cards and admitting past a check are T2.

C. Proposals only

Every hold or chain waits for the owner. The owner becomes the dispatcher.

DS-D4 · Touch forecast

A. Keyword areas

Exists, but has a pair precision of 0.23 and drives a warden to 27 % slot use.

B. Shared model-free forecast (recommended)

Cited paths, co-change expansion from a dated index, a module map, and confidence, with real files recorded at delivery. Pair F1 is 0.52 to 0.56 at zero tokens.

C. Model estimate for every card

Possibly more accurate, but costs about 3,000 tokens per card for everybody. It is better offered as a persona option whose cost the persona carries.

DS-D5 · Conflict record

A. Latest report per card

Today. No history, no partner, no attribution.

B. Append-only ledger with partner attribution (recommended)

One entry per conflict, semantic conflict or develop sync: card, partner cards that changed the conflicted files between base and tip, files, SHAs, the admitting decision ids, live or shadow. Backfilled for 30 days from git.

C. Derive from git on demand

No store, but slow, and the admitting decision is lost.

DS-D6 · Competition order

A. Alternating live from the start

Real numbers, but a weak persona costs real rework from the first day.

B. Replay, then shadow, then alternating live (recommended)

Replay is free and immediate. Shadow is near free and uses real decision-time facts. Only the top two go live, by wave window per project, behind a guard.

C. Replay and shadow only

Safe, but tokens and agent behaviour in chains are never measured for real.

DS-D7 · Criteria and value

A. Plain weighted sum

Simple, but the replay shows that it ranks a nearly sequential warden first (81).

B. Weighted sum with parallelism gate (recommended)

Min-max scores within one comparison set, default weights 30, 25, 10, 10, 10, 5, 5, 5, and a gate at 0.8 of the conflict-free bound.

C. Lexicographic

Conflicts first, then tokens. Transparent, but the owner cannot trade anything off, which contradicts weights the owner adjusts.

DS-D8 · Recut and source cut

A. Only the owner recuts

Safe and slow. Waves of 57 cards arrive in one minute.

B. Packer recuts automated waves, sources emit wave and paths (recommended)

Packages and chains for unstarted automated-source cards with a 24-hour undo, size from the source prior, and split proposals for oversized and hot collection cards.

C. Sources cut themselves

Needed in part (the prompt instruction), but a playthrough cannot see the rest of the board or the ledger.

DS-D9 · First live persona

A. Queue keeper stays live until shadow ends

No change in conflicts for several weeks.

B. Area warden on modules first, the rest in shadow (recommended)

Most robust in the replay (never more than two points behind the leader), model-free, no recut rights needed. It goes live after its shadow gate. The Packer stays in shadow with proposals only until two waves prove its tokens.

C. Packer live directly

Top value on the base replay (67 against 65), but its token gain is a proxy, and it falls behind the warden in two of the three robustness runs (69 against 70, and 59 against 66).

DS-D10 · Exposure window of the overlap check

A. Running cards only

Today's local rule. Full parallelism, but with a review and gate lag every strategy leaves 29 to 35 of 57 cards needing a sync in the replay.

B. Every in-flight card, on both claim paths (recommended)

Compare a candidate with every card of the project that has started and not integrated yet, on the local pickup and the remote claim. In the replay at lag 1 the forecast personas fall to 12 to 15 cards. Parallelism falls with them, and integrate-first wins part of it back.

C. In flight for wave cards only

Use the in-flight check only between cards of one source wave. It is cheaper in slots, but it misses collisions between a wave and owner cards, which the ledger cannot charge to anyone.

03 Recommendation

Recommended default

Build the dispatcher as a persona role whose personas name pure strategies (DS-D1 B), with a separate dispatch plan (DS-D2 B), bounded T1 rights and T2 for every bypass (DS-D3 B). Give every persona the same model-free touch forecast (DS-D4 B) and an append-only conflict ledger with partner attribution (DS-D5 B). Let personas compete by replay, then shadow, then alternating live (DS-D6 B), ranked by a weighted value with a parallelism gate (DS-D7 B). Let the Packer recut automated waves within bounds, while sources emit waves and paths (DS-D8 B). Put the forecast-module Area warden live first (DS-D9 B). Before any persona exists, make the overlap check cover every in-flight card on both claim paths (DS-D10 B).

The most important reason: on the 17 November wave, separating on forecast modules would have cut the cards needing a develop sync from 20 to 7, at no token cost and with a lead time 4 rounds above what a conflict-free plan allows. That holds only if the check covers the whole exposure window. With a lag of one card duration, the same warden leaves 31 cards with a running-only check and 12 with an in-flight check.

Expected effect on the scope numbers. Replayed, the practical personas cut rounds per card from 1.35 to 1.05 to 1.12, which is 17 to 22 % fewer rounds. Under integrate-first every avoided sync also avoids a re-review. This is the share of the 40 % rework that comes from integration. Rework from failed reviews is not touched by a dispatcher.

05 Open decisions

Each decision has a recommended working option. A promoted card uses it unless a recorded owner decision selects another.

DS-D1 · Form of a dispatcher

Is a dispatcher a model session, a pure strategy chosen by a persona, or a project setting?

  • Model session that orders the lane
  • Persona names a pure strategy in the decision library (recommended)
  • Project setting without personas

Recommended: B. Decisions are frequent, must be replayable and explainable, and must cost no tokens. Sessions write only recut texts and reports.

DS-D2 · Where the order lives

Does the dispatcher write the lane order or its own plan?

  • Write the lane order field
  • Separate dispatch plan read by the pick-gate; pins and urgent stay hard (recommended)
  • Advisory only

Recommended: B. Owner drags keep their meaning, and plans can be compared between personas.

DS-D3 · Rights and tiers

Which dispatcher actions are T1 and which need an explicit T2 grant?

  • Everything T1
  • Bounded T1 (order, hold of at most 24 hours, own edges, exclusive, bounded recut); T2 for every bypass (recommended)
  • Proposals only

Recommended: B, with the bounds in Rights.

DS-D4 · Touch forecast

What tells a dispatcher which files a card will touch?

  • Keyword areas from the enrichment
  • Shared model-free forecast: cited paths, co-change expansion, module map, confidence; real files recorded (recommended)
  • Model estimate for every card

Recommended: B. A model-assisted forecast is a persona option whose cost the persona carries.

DS-D5 · Conflict record

How are conflicts recorded so that they can be attributed and learned from?

  • Latest report per card, as today
  • Append-only ledger with partner attribution and admitting decisions, backfilled for 30 days (recommended)
  • Derive from git on demand

Recommended: B. Without the partner and the decision, a conflict cannot be charged to a dispatcher.

DS-D6 · Competition order

In which order do personas compete?

  • Alternating live from the start
  • Replay, then shadow, then alternating live by wave window per project, behind a guard (recommended)
  • Replay and shadow only

Recommended: B, with the champion and guard rules in Competition.

DS-D7 · Criteria and value

How do the criteria combine into one value?

  • Plain weighted sum
  • Weighted sum with min-max scores and a parallelism gate at 0.8; default weights 30, 25, 10, 10, 10, 5, 5, 5 (recommended)
  • Lexicographic order

Recommended: B. The owner changes weights and the gate in weight revisions on the review page.

DS-D8 · Recut and source cut

Who may recut cards, and what must sources provide?

  • Only the owner recuts
  • Packer recuts unstarted automated-source cards with undo; sources emit wave id and cited paths; size from a source prior (recommended)
  • Sources cut themselves

Recommended: B, with the rules in Cut of cards.

DS-D9 · First live persona

Which persona takes the live lease first?

  • Queue keeper until shadow ends
  • Area warden on forecast modules after its shadow gate; Packer in shadow with proposals (recommended)
  • Packer live directly

Recommended: B. It was the most robust in the replay, never more than two points behind the leader, and needs neither a model nor recut rights.

DS-D10 · Exposure window of the overlap check

Which cards does the pick-gate compare a candidate with?

  • Running cards only, as today
  • Every in-flight card of the project, from start to integration, on the local pickup and the remote claim (recommended)
  • In flight only between cards of one source wave

Recommended: B. It is the only option under which separation still works with a review and gate lag. The slot cost is accepted because efficiency comes before lead time, and integrate-first reduces it.
