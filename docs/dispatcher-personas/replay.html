Champion and challenger. The live persona is the champion. A challenger replaces it when its weighted value is at least 5 points higher over at least 100 integrated cards and two waves in shadow, and stays ahead over four alternating live waves. Guard: if the live persona's conflicts per 100 integrated cards over its last 20 cards exceed the baseline's shadow value for the same cards by 10 or more, the platform hands the lease back to the Queue keeper. This is a T1 platform action with a feed item at importance 70, and the owner can undo it.

Exposure window

The replay above integrates a card the moment its run ends. In reality a card finishes coding, then waits for the model review aspects and the batch gate (median 133 minutes for Agent Studio), and only then integrates. During that time it holds no slot, but it is still exposed: a card that starts and touches the same file will conflict with it. Today's pick-gate compares a candidate only with ActiveRuns. The replay therefore gained an integration lag, measured in card durations, and two check modes. Mode running compares a candidate with running cards, like today. Mode in flight also compares it with cards that have ended their run but have not integrated.

The bound is the planner that knows the real files, under the same lag and check mode. Under lag 1 in flight it uses 35 % of four slots, and under lag 2 it uses 24 %.

With a lag and a running-only check, separation is close to worthless. Even the planner that knows the real files sends 33 cards into a sync, because the card it waited for is still in review when the next one starts. Checking against in-flight cards restores the protection. The cost is parallelism: at lag 1 the forecast-module warden uses 28 % of four slots and needs 51 rounds instead of 27. The owner's rule that efficiency comes before lead time accepts this cost. Integrate-first, then test, shortens the lag, because the batch gate moves behind integration, and so it buys back part of the lost parallelism. The ranking of the forecast personas holds under the in-flight check: the module warden is first or tied first in both lag runs.

Five findings follow from the replay.

  1. Separating cards on a forecast cuts the cards needing a develop sync by 45 to 65 %, at a lead time close to the conflict-free bound. The bound is 23 rounds against 26 to 27 for the practical personas.
  2. The forecast-module Area warden is the most robust. It is first in two of the four runs, tied first in one, and two points behind the Packer (65 against 67) in the base run. No other persona stays within two points of the leader in every run. It needs no model and no recut rights.
  3. Packaging helps the token side (6 fewer runs and reviews) but not the conflict side. With six slots and size-weighted durations it is worse than the warden. It earns its place through measured tokens, which only shadow and live can show.
  4. The Historian starts cold on this wave and gains a little over the Surveyor. Its value depends on the ledger, which does not exist yet.
  5. Every strategy needs the overlap check to cover the whole exposure window, from start to integration, and on both claim paths. Without that, no persona can keep its promise. This is the first slice (DS-0), and it pays off before any persona exists. Today's order with the prompt-path scope and an in-flight check already falls to 13 cards at lag 1, as an upper bound.
