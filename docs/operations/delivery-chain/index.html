§1 The chain and where it breaks

The delivery chain is one loop with one release arm. An agent delivers on a fenced ref, a deterministic gate builds and tests it, a review judges it, canonical integration merges it into the integration branch, acceptance closes the card, and a separate promotion train publishes a pinned candidate to main. Under the loop lie two layers that the loop cannot see: the control plane that moves the loop forward, and the substrate that actually executes it.

Every failure this Dossier records sits at exactly one place in that picture, and almost every one of them is silent. A self-running fleet does not fail with an exception and a shouting user. It fails with a feed that stops moving, a closed auto-pickup with lastRejection=null, a breaker on mode=paused with an empty halt reason, a "Delivered" badge over work that never reached the integration branch. Six different causes surfaced under the same sentence, "System appears stalled". The visible work is therefore not the fix, it is making the silence legible and giving it an owner that survives a session.

Two boundaries that hold the rest of the document together. First, a stage boundary: a fresh recovery round may rewrite delivery identity because it records a new fence and new attribution before review, whereas canonical integration must preserve the identity that the acceptance evidence already names, and promotion may not change it at all. That is not one Git policy applied inconsistently three times, it is three different relationships to the same evidence. Second, a proof boundary: lane, pipeline and Git may each tell a different story, so "completed" and "delivered" are statements that must be derived from target-branch presence, not from the lane a card happens to sit in.

§3 Alternatives

The Git policy per stage is settled (§2). What remains genuinely open is the shape of the robustness programme: which property from Fig. 1 is attacked first, and therefore what the chain looks like in three months. Three candidates, deliberately different in approach.

The three are not exclusive. A and B are sequenced, not chosen against each other; C is the outcome if neither is sequenced. The decision the order forces is which one pays first, and that matters because a healing orchestrator that acts on a wrong classification heals into the void.

§4 Recommendation

A, then B, then lifecycle ownership. Build the diagnosis contract and the cause breaker now, because they are days of work against measurable daily waste; run the isolation migration owned by AGT-W51 next, because it is the only thing that makes a green gate a proof; then give processes, worktrees and leases an owner and turn the operator recipes into executable healings that the watcher may run inside declared authority limits.

Keep the hybrid Git policy and make its boundary explicit. The stage boundary from §2 is the load-bearing part: renew identity on requeue, preserve it at canonical integration, never mutate after the gate. Nothing in the robustness programme is allowed to weaken it. Changing canonical integration to squash or to an unmapped rebase would require a new acceptance evidence contract first, and that contract does not exist.

Move the first recoverable bounce out of the session. A reviewed card in conflict-skipped is a typed, structured situation: branch, result ref, failure code, epoch, attempt, conflict paths and retry count are all already recorded. No model is needed to append a precise steer, supersede the failed generation, pick the safe mechanical route and queue the card ready. Give that one deterministic round per operator review epoch to a restart-safe backend rule built on the services that already exist, and reserve a strong guardian for the second round, where the evidence is contradictory or the conflict is genuinely semantic. Model authority ends at authoring the explanation and the proposed steer; the lane change and the Git mutation stay with pure policy.

Make the board status model honest in the same pass. The diagnosis contract decides whether a failure is the delivery's fault; the status contract decides whether a card may claim it delivered. Both are the same question asked at different stages, and neither survives alone: a correct classification that still lets an unintegrated card reach "Delivered" has not removed the class of error this chain produces.

Sequencing note. Wave one is buildable in days and would have bounded every incident of the last two weeks to a single attempt. Wave two carries the operational decisions and belongs to AGT-W51. Wave three presupposes that situations are recognised correctly, which is exactly what wave one delivers. Throughput work, guard tests first and the full suite only after they pass, runs in parallel and its larger form is decided in the Gates Dossier, not here.

The shape is the problem, not only the size: a cold rebase-only round had a median of 12,112,130 tokens and 1,258.8 seconds, which is 3.9% more tokens and 26.1% longer than a first coding run. A mechanical continuation that costs more than the original work is the wrong shape. Session continuation and model substitution overlap and must not be added together.

E3 · A card can be completed and have delivered nothing

Incident of 2026-08-09, reconstructed from task folders, the backend log and Git history. Two deliveries existed only on salvage fences. One of them, a gate fix, was set to 6-completed after the runner reported an incomplete result envelope; thirty-eight minutes later the accepted-integration worker processed it with outcome NoTaskBranch in 1,075 ms and reported nothing back to the card, which stayed completed with integration=null. Neither break alone would have done this: the envelope failure without the silent acceptance would have been a visible escalation, and the silent acceptance without the envelope failure would have found a branch and integrated normally.

The concealment mattered as much as the breaks. The accepted-integration backstop repeated the failed integrations every fifteen minutes while reporting integrated N accepted delivery(s) with a counter rising to ten, because "integrated" also counted AlreadyMerged repeats and failed attempts. Anyone reading the log saw normal operation.

What worked: the salvage fence push before teardown preserved all four work states without loss, and the escalation categories named the missing envelope fields exactly. Recovery was two card-scoped merges, 6ea7f7978 and 720129404, verified with a baseline counter-run that proved no new breaks. The three hardening cards AGT-2543 (acceptance invariant), AGT-2544 (envelope duty with automatic requeue and the fence push as contract) and AGT-2545 (honest telemetry with an alarm after thirty minutes without successful integration) each close one of the three.

E5 · Recurring classes from the rolling supervision log

The live improvement log ran from 2026-08-18 under a discipline worth keeping: every observation is reconciled against in-flight cards and existing Dossiers before it may become work, and a card is cut on the second sighting, not the first. Synthesised, the log produced eight recurring shapes rather than a list of incidents.

  1. Session-bound control loops die with their session. Five sightings across thirteen days before the platform rail was cut. The band-aid, recreating the cron each session, failed every night.
  2. Transport death on the control plane. Five tunnel deaths, root-caused on 08-23 and then immediately re-broken by the hardening itself, because a port check exited non-zero when the port was free and the guard read that as "remote unreachable".
  3. Silent gate closure. A settings edit closed auto-pickup for a whole project for five days with no banner and no rejection reason.
  4. Hollow restarts and stale binaries. A restart that never replaced the process, and an integrated fix that stayed invisible because the runner binary on the host was ten days old.
  5. Shared-resource exhaustion. One account limit produced 32 escalations and a zero-claim morning, and the breaker that paused carried no reason.
  6. Unbounded loops against an unachievable gate. A review aspect that no single delivery could satisfy kept post-processing full while the review layer was healthy.
  7. Integration that never reaches the remote. 705 claims and 0 acceptances in one night because the merge into the local integration branch succeeded while the push was skipped as lineage-blocked, so acceptance read the remote and saw pending forever.
  8. Half-installed tooling. Package present, shims gone, twice.

The log also measured the carousel rather than describing it. Over thirty days in Agent Studio: 5,842 lane moves, 1,187 of them backward across 259 tasks; operator requeue 354 moves amounting to 95 days of cumulative rework at a median of 1.9 hours each; escalation requeue 187 moves, lease recovery 263, quality loop 163, gate failure 112, integration recovery 42. Cycle-time medians from the same period put post-processing wait at 1.2 days and human review at 2.1 days against a lead time of 3.8 days. Most rework is the chain reacting to its own movement, and the two dominant waits are both queues in front of a human, not compute.

Cause attribution in that taxonomy is inferred from neighbouring ledger rows. Writing cause and reason onto the lane-change rows would make it exact; until then treat the split between categories as indicative and the totals as solid.

E6 · The status model the chain runs on

Inventory of 2026-07-31. Twelve persisted lane values: eight regular lanes, the escalation lane, and three additive lanes of which one is retired, one is drain-only and one has no productive writer at all. The central state machine validates only that the target lane is known, so any lane is technically reachable from any other and no domain edge matrix exists. Acceptance happens before integration: the lane change lands first, while merge, gate and push run afterwards in the background and cannot take the visible completion back. Archive has no integration guard, so an operator or management sweep can archive cards that were never integrated.

The vocabulary that a guarded model needs is already drafted: integrated and not-applicable permit review, accept and archive; pending and partial permit none of them; no-branch, conflict, gate-failed and error route to escalation and allow archive only by explicit override. What is missing is not the vocabulary but the decision about where the guard sits, which is why it appears in §6 rather than §2.

Decision, 2026-09-25, operator: A. Pull request approval is separate, and human review starts after the merge.

D7 · How is a mechanical rebase round executed?

Decision, 2026-09-25: Adopt A. A fenced coding generation records its captured session and usage on the task. The next integration-recovery generation receives a compact base-SHA, conflict-path, steer and verification delta. Resume is single-use and requires matching task, provider, clean-context identity, repository, worktree and delivery-branch lineage. Mismatch, stale or missing session, semantic conflict and failed deterministic gate produce a typed, policy-qualified fresh-run decision. A resumed round is limited to 1,211,213 observed tokens and 300 seconds. The 17.6 to 19.8 quota-point saving remains an estimate pending live pilot evidence; it is not additive with B.

Decision, 2026-09-25, operator: A. Resume is bounded by lineage checks, a compact delta and token cap, with a typed fresh-run fallback.

Task Server completion and claim contract: A resumed mechanical fallback is a typed completion that returns the task to Ready and records the reason on the fenced session entry. Both direct claims and accepted host permits qualify a pending delta before runner admission. The next claim after a fallback reads that reason and selects a policy-qualified fresh route.
