Decision cards

A decision request today is an ordinary task card with "decision needed" somewhere in its prose. The decider cannot see that a decision is wanted, has nothing to click, and the dependent work sits idle. This Dossier describes decisions as first-class objects on the board: typed, visible, decidable in one action, and connected to the work they unblock.

§1Situation

On 2026-09-13 the first develop → main promotion under the new gate succeeded and the Stable update was refused by the release preflight. The follow-up card AGT-2792 is a decision request: two options, an operator must choose, nothing can start before the choice. On the board it is a coding task in lane 1-preparation. The operator's reaction, verbatim: the card is a decision task, but there is no way to decide; the interface and the calls to action are missing; the type is invisible; the workflow is unclear; before the decision nothing moves.

The shape recurs whenever an agent reaches a fork it must not take alone: a dependency strategy, a version number, a breaking contract change, deleting thousands of branches. Five open cards carry such requests in prose today (§6). Each of them waits for a human who has no signal that they are waited for.

§3Target picture

A decision is an object with its own lifecycle. It is requested by an agent or a service, it waits for a named decider, it is decided with one action and a rationale, and its result is applied to the work it blocks. Every step leaves a record.

Operator position, 2026-09-14: separate the service from its hosts. A Dossier decision and a card decision share one mechanism and nothing else. A Dossier is started by a decision and links the work that follows; it has no execution cycle of its own. A card has the full one: lane, claim, lease, runs, review, integration. So the decision service stays a plain service, host-agnostic, and each host binds it to its own lifecycle. What a decision does after it is taken therefore differs by host: on a Dossier it spawns or links cards, on a card it unblocks that card's own execution. The relationship between the two is one Dossier to many cards, and that relation is part of the working model, not an implementation detail. It is examined in its own Dossier together with the question of how Dossiers and Epics relate.

Three properties make this work in practice. The options are fields, not markdown, so the board can render them and the decider can click them. The decider is named, so the inbox can address a person or a role instead of hoping someone reads the lane. The dependants are linked, so "blocked" is a computed state rather than a convention in the prompt.

§5Recommendation

A, with the Dossier decision service as the shared backbone. A single fork with a handful of options belongs on the board where the blocked work lives, not in a document. The card carries the options; the decision service records the choice in the format that already exists; the wiki keeps the ADR-style record; concept promotion and the new "apply" step share one mechanism for turning a decision into cards. Multi-question concepts remain Dossiers (B) and can spawn decision cards for the single forks they surface.

What the decider experiences. A card with a "Decision" badge and their name on it appears in the inbox and on the board. Opening it shows the question, the options with consequences, and the recommendation. One click on an option plus an optional sentence records the decision. The dependent cards lose their "blocked by" marker and enter 2-ready, or new cards appear from the chosen option. Reopening is one action with a note.

C is not recommended as a product answer, but its cheapest element, a visible lane placement, is worth keeping until A ships: decision cards stay in 1-preparation and never enter a runner lane.

§7Workflow

  1. Request

    An agent, the runner (outcome Blocked with a fork), failure intervention, or a Dossier creates a decision card: question, two to four options (id, label, consequences, effort, risk), optional recommendation with reason, decider, due date, dependants. API: POST /api/tasks with kind: decision and an options payload.

  2. Pending

    Board and list show the badge and the decider; the project header counts open decisions; the inbox lists them beside Dossier decisions. Dependants show "blocked by AGT-nnnn" and cannot be claimed. A reminder fires when the due date passes.

  3. Decide

    The decider opens the card, chooses an option, adds a rationale. POST /api/tasks/{id}/decision records option, rationale, X-Client-Id and timestamp, moves the card to decided, and writes the record to the project wiki.

  4. Apply

    If an implementation card is linked, its prompt receives a decision block (question, chosen option, rationale, link) and it moves to 2-ready. If none exists, cards are created from the chosen option's requirements, the same way concept promotion creates them.

  5. Reopen

    POST /api/tasks/{id}/decision/reopen with a note re-blocks dependants and returns the card to pending; the record keeps both entries.
